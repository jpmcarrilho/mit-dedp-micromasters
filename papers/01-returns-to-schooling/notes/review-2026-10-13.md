## Findings, most important first

1. **The 2022 exception is explained wrongly in the paper and in `02_checks.R`.** IBGE's *Rendimento de todas as fontes 2024* report states that its annual series uses first-visit files for 2012, 2014, 2019, 2023 and 2024 and fifth-visit files for 2020–2022 ("Dados consolidados de primeiras visitas em 2012, 2014, 2019, 2023 e 2024, e de quintas visitas no período 2020-2022"). The published 2022 value therefore comes from a different interview of different households, and the 3.3% gap is a visit difference, not a stale SIDRA cell or a revised microdata file. Fix: reword Section 3.3, Section 7 and the comment block in `R/02_checks.R`; optionally run check A for 2022 against the fifth-visit file (`interview = 5`) to close the loop. Severity: medium (a reviewer would catch it; the estimates are unaffected).

2. **The "factor of three" is right but incompletely explained.** Within a single year the design-based SE is 1.3 times the HC1 SE (2023: 0.00104 vs 0.00078 on the weighted regression). The pooled ratio of 3.4 arises because the pooled design also treats people in the same tract in different years as correlated, and the master sample rotates slowly: 52% of 2012 tracts are still sampled in 2015, 18% in 2019, about 1% in 2022; 64% of 2022 tracts are sampled again in 2023. The pooled design has 45,424 distinct stratum-tract clusters for 145,271 year-tracts. Unequal weights add to the gap (column 7 is also unweighted). Fix: add a weighted-HC1 column to Table 6, report the single-year ratio, and reword the paragraph in Section 6 and the sentence in the abstract ("in the pooled design"). Severity: medium (interpretation, not numbers).

3. **Two sentences about the survey are inaccurate.** "It is this first-visit file that IBGE uses for its own annual labour-income releases" holds except for 2020–2022. "Treats the same tract in different years as one cluster, which is the conservative choice for a survey that reuses its master sample" should say that tracts are replaced gradually and that the 2025 file carries new stratum codes for 31% of tracts. Severity: low.

4. **No selection correction, while the three closest Brazilian papers use one** (Ribeiro et al. 2025; Cota et al. 2025; Pereira et al. 2013). The paper says so in Section 7, but a reviewer will ask. The first-visit file has the household variables a Heckman probit needs (children, other household income). Fix: a Heckman-corrected column in the robustness table, with the usual caveat about exclusion restrictions. Severity: medium for a journal, low for a working paper.

5. **Positioning against the literature.** The draft does not cite Ribeiro, Nakabashi and Barros Jr. (2025) or Cota, Pinho Neto and Alves (2025), the two papers closest to it, and it presents design-based inference as a distinguishing feature although Ribeiro et al. also account for the design. The contribution should be stated as: year-by-year completed-level premia with design-based intervals, reproduction of official aggregates, the explicit inference comparison, all occupied workers including the self-employed, and the replication package. See `literature-review.md`, Section 8, for where each citation goes. Severity: medium (credibility).

6. **Robustness worth adding, already computed here:** the hours 20–70 variant and the alternative reference group for the level premia (both strengthen the results). Severity: low.

7. **Minor code points.** The 95% intervals in the robustness table use 1.96 rather than the design t quantile (immaterial at 44,580 degrees of freedom). The trimming quantiles are unweighted; say so in the table note. `reg_year` is computed but unused. The comment in `02_checks.R` about the 2022 exception must change with finding 1. None affects a number in the paper.

## What I could not verify

- Bibliographic details were typed from memory and from search snippets; spot-check volume, issue and page numbers before release.
- The PNADcIBGE package's reading of the fixed-width files is trusted; the official-aggregate checks are the evidence that it is right.
- The fifth-visit explanation for 2022 rests on the report's footnote; a run against the 2022 fifth-visit file would confirm it.
- Nothing in the pipeline tests whether the OLS gradient is close to a causal return; the paper does not claim it is.

## Verdict

The numbers can be trusted for what they claim to be: precisely measured descriptive gradients with correct sampling inference, validated against IBGE's own aggregates and reproducible from public files. The paper is worth sharing as a working paper once findings 1, 2, 3 and 5 are fixed, which is an afternoon's work; findings 4 and 6 would take one or two more days and would make it defensible at a Brazilian journal. It should not go out before the 2022 explanation and the literature positioning are corrected, because both are the kind of thing a referee or an admissions reader notices first.
